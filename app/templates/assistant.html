What she can do

The same things you can, through the same code the screens use

It reads

  • Where the project stands — dates, float, planned against earned, hours and CPI.
  • Any deliverable: its dates, progress, team, float, what it waits on.
  • What moved between two dates, and what this week needs.
  • Both registers, including as at a past date.

She changes — after you approve

  • Records progress, by percentage or by workflow step.
  • Moves start and submission dates, cascading what waits on them.
  • Adds and removes dependencies; adds holidays.
  • Raises and closes actions in either register.
  • Minutes a meeting from what you type — the meeting, who was there, and the items numbered with owners, dates and what each one affects — and corrects items already minuted.

She produces

  • A PowerPoint of the work done between any two dates.
  • Word minutes on the practice's template, header, footer and signature blocks.

She takes you places

  • “Take me to the schedule” opens the schedule.
  • “Print the budget as a PDF” opens that tab's print dialog.

Where the limits are

Worth reading once

  • Nothing changes until you press Apply. A model can be confidently wrong about which deliverable “the design basis one” is, and the cost of that should be a sentence to correct, not a programme to unpick.
  • She cannot do more than you can. Every change goes through the same function a form posts to, and applying takes the same role as editing the screen.
  • She only sees this project. The project comes from the address bar, not from anything said in the chat.
  • Check what she tells you. She reads real figures, but she is a language model reading them — for anything that matters, the tab it came from is one click away.
  • Every conversation is kept and goes to Drive each night as a text file, so how she is being used is something anybody can look at.
