        Berth by berth

        Each bar is a ship alongside, with her flag; the amber before it is time waiting at anchor. The line is the radar's time.

        How a ship is given a berth

        First what she needs, then the rule for who goes first.

        1. Long enough: the berth is at least her length plus {{ '%.0f' | format(margin) }} m for her lines. A ship longer than one berth takes two next to each other.
        2. Deep enough: the depth alongside is her draught plus {{ '%.0f' | format(ukc * 100) }}% under the keel (PIANC). Deep ships can also be held for the high tide.
        3. Set up for her cargo: container ships to berths with ship-to-shore or mobile cranes, RoRo and car carriers to a berth with a ramp, bulk to a bulk berth; any ship to a berth set to "Any ship".
        4. Cranes: her time alongside is what is to be worked over the cranes she gets (about one crane for every 110 m of ship, up to the berth's: {{ rates['STS'] | int }} boxes an hour each for ship-to-shore cranes, {{ rates['MHC'] | int }} for mobile ones), plus three hours to berth, lash and sail.
        5. The berth asked for: a line's own or preferred berth is used when it is free at the same time as another.
        6. Then who goes first, by the rule below. Other rules ports use: priority to ships with dangerous goods or passengers, tidal windows for the deepest ships, and a fixed berth for each line (dedicated terminals).
